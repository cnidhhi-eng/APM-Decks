The Problem

The last mile is the riskiest mile

I take Uber daily in Bangalore. During peak hours, I've noticed a consistent and alarming pattern: as my ride approaches its destination, the driver's phone lights up with incoming ride requests. What follows is not passive notification — it's active browsing. The driver starts scrolling, comparing, tapping. Eyes off the road. Both hands engaged. The passenger — me — is still in the car.

This isn't a driver problem. It's a design problem. Uber's current system optimises for driver efficiency at the cost of passenger safety in the most critical window of every trip.

🚨

Passenger safety

Driver distraction peaks when the passenger is still in the vehicle and vulnerable.

⚖️

Wrong incentive design

Drivers are rewarded for fast acceptance but penalised by nothing for distracted driving.

📱

UX forces bad behaviour

Showing multiple options during motion forces visual comparison — impossible to do safely while driving.

The Solution

SafeSwitch — context-aware ride handoff

SafeSwitch uses three signals Uber already has — GPS speed, ride ETA, and app state — to completely redesign how and when ride requests reach the driver. The fix is not fewer notifications. It's smarter ones.

1
While moving

AI voice notification — no screen required

When the vehicle is moving above 10 km/h, incoming ride requests are delivered as voice alerts: "New ride — 2.4 km away, ₹180, heading to Koramangala. Say accept or skip." Driver responds hands-free. Zero screen interaction while in motion.

2
At signal / stopped

Visual pop-up activates only when vehicle is stationary

GPS speed drops to 0? That's the trigger. The full ride-selection UI appears only when the driver is stopped — at a signal, in traffic, or parked. Visual comparison becomes safe again because the vehicle isn't moving.

3
Ride ends

Curated shortlist — not a firehose

The moment the ride completes, instead of showing every available request simultaneously, SafeSwitch surfaces the top 3 options ranked by distance, surge, and driver preference history. One decision, not ten. Faster for the driver, safer for everyone.

Success Metrics

How I'd measure if this works

Simple signals, first 8 weeks of pilot.

↓
Screen interactions during motion
Primary safety signal. Track taps/swipes on ride-request UI while speed > 10 km/h. Target: 80% reduction vs baseline.
↑
Driver acceptance rate
Does voice + curated list hurt or help acceptance? Hypothesis: curated top 3 is faster than scrolling through 8 options.
↓
Time-to-accept after ride ends
Curated shortlist should reduce decision time. Target: 30% faster than current firehose experience.
↑
Passenger safety rating
Track end-of-trip ratings that mention smooth/safe ride. Proxy for actual safety improvement.
🎯
North Star
Zero screen interactions while moving

This single number captures everything SafeSwitch is trying to do. Uber completes millions of rides daily in India. If even 30% of ride-end windows go from 4+ screen taps to zero, the safety impact is enormous — and the driver experience is actually better. Faster decisions, less cognitive load, same earnings.

Roadmap

Phased rollout

Start with the highest-signal, lowest-risk change. Layer on intelligence.

Phase 1 · 3 weeks
Speed-gated UI
  • Lock ride-request pop-up when GPS speed > 10 km/h
  • Basic voice TTS alert for incoming rides
  • A/B test in Bangalore & Mumbai
  • Measure screen-tap drop
Phase 2 · 6 weeks
Voice accept/reject
  • Wake word or button-triggered voice
  • Natural language: "accept", "skip", "next"
  • Fallback to haptic confirm
  • Measure acceptance rate vs control
Phase 3 · 10 weeks
Smart shortlist
  • ML ranking: distance + surge + driver preference
  • Top 3 curated rides shown at trip end
  • One-tap accept for best match
  • National rollout if P2 metrics hold
Phase 4 · 16 weeks
Proactive routing
  • Predict likely next ride zone from current route
  • Pre-position driver intent before trip ends
  • Driver earnings dashboard with safety score
  • Global rollout framework
